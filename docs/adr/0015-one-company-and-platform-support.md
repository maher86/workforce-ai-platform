# ADR 0015: One company per management account, separate platform support

Status: accepted owner clarification, 2026-11-21; live operator bootstrap and physical usability acceptance are separate.

The platform remains multi-tenant. Each ordinary company owner or administrator account belongs to one company. This supersedes the multi-company account selector described in ADR0014 and the older permitted multi-membership management UX. A company can have several administrators, each using a separate named account. Owners cannot create another company with the same account or invite an account already bound elsewhere. Employees continue to use their device-resolved membership, not management company signup.

users.company_id anchors management authorization. User-row locks serialize company provisioning and administrator enrollment. TenantAccess checks the anchor even if a historical membership in another tenant still exists. Team and overview return only the selected authorized company; no switcher is rendered. Public registration cannot set company_id, platform_admin, employee_only or a role. A platform account cannot join a company. Database constraints prevent a platform account from carrying company_id.

The additive migration backfills accounts with exactly one active management company. Ambiguous legacy accounts remain unbound and fail closed instead of choosing a company silently; the operator must resolve them with the account owner's selection. The owner explicitly selected the QR test workspace for the current local account; only that management anchor was changed and audited. Other companies, memberships, reports, employee devices and evidence are preserved. The CLI company:bind-account requires an existing active owner/manager membership, expected prior binding and explicit --apply; dry-run is the default. It does not create permissions or delete records. Keep real identifiers and email addresses out of public documentation.

Platform administrators use a separate identity provisioned through platform:create-admin with a unique unused email and an interactive password. The command refuses to promote any existing customer account. Nothing is provisioned automatically or through public registration. Passwords are hashed, never printed, placed in Git or shared with customers. Email verification, confirmed two-factor authentication and password confirmation within ten minutes are required for /platform. Normal customer accounts cannot enter this console. Platform administrators are not automatically customer members and TenantAccess denies their normal company routes and reporting actions.

The initial console lists companies and offers a fifteen-minute diagnostic session for a selected company. The operator supplies a support reason, encrypted at rest. Each diagnostic grant and view is audited under the platform operator's own identity. Grants are scoped to actor and tenant and expire; another platform operator cannot reuse the URL. Responses contain only company metadata, aggregate submission/processing counts and recent failure codes/report IDs. They omit employee names, phones, audio, images, original speech, translations and service evidence. Counts are operational activity, not verified completion or productivity. This is not customer impersonation, a master password, an employee device-proof bypass or a general data editor. Additional customer-data access or support writes require their own scoped authorization, audit and retention design.

Platform support metadata remains private/no-store under existing middleware. No channel adapter, AI prompt, audio pipeline or WhatsApp integration changes. Tests cover forged signup privilege fields, second-company creation/enrollment denial, historical extra memberships/direct tenant IDs, platform role/MFA/password gates, missing employee evidence, actor-scoped expiry, audits and guarded CLI behavior. Physical operator onboarding, mail delivery, recovery, least-privilege infrastructure access and production monitoring remain deployment requirements.

# Pricing and AI funding proposal

Status: owner-requested public plan preview, 2026-11-21. The owner selected AED and employee-based subscriptions; no amounts were specified. Final retail rates, allowances, payment provider and overage policy are not approved. Automated billing and company-specific OpenAI connections are not implemented by this change.

The landing presents Basic, Medium and Enterprise in Arabic and English. Basic is positioned for small teams, Medium for growing teams, Enterprise for negotiated volume/operations. Existing employee activation, original/English reports and manager capabilities are common baseline features, not new tier-enforced restrictions. Allowances are proposals, not active entitlements. No invented employee caps, included tokens, discounts, unlimited use, support SLA or purchasable prices. The CTA creates a normal company account; it does not record a plan, take payment or change AI configuration.

For comparison only, the proposed employee bands are Basic1–10, Medium11–50 and Enterprise51+. These are explicitly labeled proposed, not approved hard caps or current enforcement. A bounded employee-count input highlights the suggested band, not a payment amount. AED per employee per month is the intended subscription unit; the employee rate remains unset. Finalize the bands and whether seats mean active/provisioned/billable employees, peak seats versus proration and reactivation behavior before implementing actual invoices. Existing database limits are unchanged. Do not silently bill every name/device ever created.

## Commercial model

Use a platform subscription plus separately disclosed AI consumption. Do not present the platform as owning a proprietary speech model: current processing uses OpenAI through the platform's server credential. A simple customer statement should show recording duration, processing usage and charges, while preserving the underlying provider/model dimensions for reconciliation. Number of reports and processing time are not token usage.

Before live billing, approve monthly platform fees, currency/tax handling, exact allowances, model tariffs with effective dates, margin/markup, what happens at a spending cap, refunds and retry policy. Enterprise agreements must define their own volume and support terms. Do not publish invented or unapproved financial commitments just because a plan card exists.

Speech transcription and English translation are separate provider stages. Provider audio usage/pricing may differ from text input/output pricing. Text input, output, cached-input and audio token categories must not be treated as one flat-priced pool. Per-minute estimates can help customers understand recording usage, but must not be added on top of the same already-priced audio tokens. Rate calculation must follow the selected model's actual billable dimensions and avoid counting usage detail subsets twice. Images attached in the current text mode are private evidence, not automatically billable image-analysis calls. stage_duration_ms is processing latency, not recording length or a chargeable duration.

Conceptually, a statement is the platform subscription plus the sum of each billable stage's measured units multiplied by its versioned tariffs, minus approved allowances/credits. There are no numeric tariffs in this preview, and no assumption that a million input tokens costs the same as a million output tokens. Do not derive exact token usage from transcript length or make speculative cost estimates into invoices.

## Two funding options

1. Platform-managed OpenAI: recommended first rollout. The platform operates the provider connection and owns the provider bill; companies would receive separately disclosed usage charges under an approved future price schedule. Keep credentials only on the backend, never in the APK/browser or public repository. Existing runtime behavior remains unchanged.
2. Company-funded OpenAI: planned, informational comparison only. The company would fund provider usage directly while paying for the platform separately. This needs a provider-supported customer-controlled/delegated connection and terms/security review. Do not ask customers to share a personal key or treat key transfer/resale as the product. No key entry, secret storage, tenant credential routing, gateway or provider switching is implemented here. If a supported workload identity/customer gateway is adopted later, design its authorization, rotation, revocation, strict outbound origins and tenant boundaries before enabling it. Never silently fall back to the platform credential when a company's connection fails.

## Billing implementation prerequisites

Existing ReportOutput usage metadata records successful processing stages. It is not a financial ledger: a paid request may fail before its result is persisted, retries can incur separate provider cost, and exports/invoices/refunds/currency and plan entitlements do not exist yet.

Implement an append-only tenant-scoped usage ledger with provider request ID, model, stage, report/source version, funding/credential reference (never the secret), unit categories, tariff version, decimal currency amounts and reconciliation state. Distinguish provider spend from customer billable spend; approve which retries the platform absorbs and never debit twice for the same ledger event. Preserve original usage/provenance while handling corrections and credits as new records. Define atomic budget reservations, finalization/release and race-safe caps before showing a guaranteed spending limit. Reconcile uncertain/failed requests with provider usage/cost records rather than fabricating zero cost. Test two tenants, concurrency, duplicate deliveries, late/stale jobs, provider failures, tariff changes and no-secret logging. These are prerequisites, not claims that the current quota fields implement monetary budgets.

No pricing interaction makes paid provider calls, edits customer data or changes the one-company account rule. A company-specific billing/connection settings page is a separate future task requiring tenancy and fidelity review.
